iHow to export it from Enlighten
  1. Open Enlighten (app or enlighten.enphaseenergy.com) and go to the reports or data download section of your system.
  2. Create a report with Energy Produced, Energy Consumed, Exported to Grid and Imported from Grid. Consumption and grid values need consumption meters (CTs) on your gateway.
  3. Pick the whole period since installation, daily or in 15-minute/hourly steps, and download it as CSV.

A full year of data means nothing has to be estimated. With less, the page estimates the missing months.

i-DE hourly consumption

Your home use, hour by hour, for a full year before the panels.

Optional

iWhere to get this file
  1. Sign in to the i-DE customer area and open your consumption page: i-de.es · Área privada · Consumo.
  2. Choose the hourly consumption for a period and set it from 1 January to 31 December of a full year, ideally one before your panels were installed (for you, 2025).
  3. Download it as CSV. The file is named like consumo_periodo_01-01-2025_31-12-2025.csv.

i-DE is the distributor in your area; other distributors (or datadis.es) offer the same hourly CSV. Menu names on their sites may differ slightly.

iWhat this file is used for

A full year of hourly readings from before the panels shows your real home use month by month and at what hours you use it. The rate finder uses it automatically to build your consumption profile; nothing else to enter.

iHow missing months and days are estimated

Missing days in a month you have data for (for example August, which starts on the 10th, or days the gateway didn't report): solar for those days follows the seasonal curve; home use per day is the average of the days you do have in that month; the share of solar you use at home and how your grid purchases split between P1, P2 and P3 follow the measured days. The month shows as partly measured, and the solar chart stacks the measured part (green) and the estimated part (blue).

Months with no data at all: solar follows one smooth seasonal curve that peaks at the June solstice and is fitted to all the days you have measured. Home use comes from your i-DE file when you have one, otherwise from the consumption profile. How much of that solar you use at home follows the day-by-day pattern of the measured month with the most similar sunshine.

A day counts as measured when the report has the whole day: any value for daily reports, or at least 22 hours for hourly or 15-minute reports.

iHow the consumption profile works

Months with real home use (Enphase, or your provider's hourly file or readings from before the panels) keep their value. Every other month follows the profile, scaled so it lines up with the months you have data for. Edit any month as a % of your average month.

Grid purchases by time period

What you buy from the grid each month, split into P1 punta, P2 llano and P3 valle. This is what the rates price.

iWhich months are measured

With an i-DE file covering a full year, every month is measured: your real hourly use sets what you buy from the grid in each period. Without it, the status follows Enphase: measured when Enphase covers every day of the month, partly measured when it covers only some days (the rest are estimated), and estimated otherwise. In the yearly consumption profile the status always follows Enphase, because only Enphase measures solar production and export.

iWhen you bought from the grid

Daily rows don't say at what hour you imported. Weekends and national holidays are all P3 valle; weekday imports are split with these shares. The defaults (22 / 17 / 61) come from your Sep–Oct bill, which matches this data to within 1 kWh.

iHow the deductions and payback are calculated

IRPF deduction: the amount you deduct in your income tax return. It is counted in year 1.

IBI deduction: the total discount on your property tax over all the years it applies (in Colmenar Viejo, 50% of the yearly IBI for 4 years). It is spread evenly over years 1 to 4.

Net cost = installation cost − IRPF deduction − IBI deduction. Payback is the year in which your running balance turns positive.

25-year outlook: the balance starts at minus the installation cost. Each year adds that year's savings, which shrink with the panels' output (1% less production per year by default), plus the deductions. Electricity prices are kept at today's rates.

Equivalent annual return is the yearly interest rate that would turn the installation cost into the same stream of savings and deductions over 25 years (the internal rate of return). It lets you compare the panels with a savings account or an investment fund.

iHow measured and estimated months are built

Measured months use your Enphase readings; partial months are scaled to the full month. For every other month, home use comes from your provider's hourly file (or typed readings) from before the installation when you have them, otherwise from the consumption profile. The hourly file also gives the real split of your use between P1, P2 and P3 and how much of it happens in daylight, which decides what solar can offset, and solar production follows a smooth seasonal curve that peaks at the June solstice and is fitted to the months Enphase measured. The day-by-day pattern of that measured month decides how much of the solar you use directly; it scales with home use but can't exceed what the panels produce.

How the bill is calculated

what goes into each monthly bill, the yearly estimate and its limits

What the page needs. Your Enphase report, which gives solar production, home use, export and grid purchases for every day it covers. Optionally, the hourly consumption file from i-DE for a full year before your panels, which gives your real home use for every month and at what hours you use it. There is no manual entry: everything is built from these files.

Monthly bill, per rate. Power has two periods with their own contracted kW and their own price: P1 punta (8–24h on weekdays) and P2 valle (0–8h, weekends and holidays). Power = P1 kW × days × P1 €/kW·day + P2 kW × days × P2 €/kW·day. Many rates charge the same price for both. Energy = kWh bought in each period × €/kWh (P1 punta 10–14h and 18–22h weekdays, P2 llano 8–10h, 14–18h, 22–24h weekdays, P3 valle nights and weekends; single-price rates charge one price). Surplus credit = kWh exported × the rate's pre-tax €/kWh, capped at what the rate lets it offset (energy, or energy and power). Then the electricity tax (Impuesto Especial sobre la Electricidad, 5,11269632% of power + energy − credit, with a legal minimum of 1 €/MWh consumed), and IVA on that plus bono social, meter rental and any monthly fee.

How the year is built. The rates are compared on twelve calendar months. With a full year of Enphase data, every month is measured. With less, the missing months are estimated: with the i-DE file, home use for those months is real and only solar is estimated; without it, home use follows the consumption profile you choose, scaled to the months you have measured.

Estimates. Solar for a missing month follows a smooth seasonal curve that peaks at the June solstice, fitted to your measured months. How much of that solar you use directly follows the day-by-day pattern of your most similar measured month, and only the daylight part of your use (from the i-DE file) can be covered by it. Partial months are scaled to the full month. Yearly cost is the sum of the twelve bills, minus any virtual-battery credit.

Limits. Rate data comes from simuladorfacturaluz.es and is refreshed weekly. Only household rates are compared: plans that require a high annual consumption are left out, as are indexed rates (PVPC, OMIE) and rates with their own time bands, which can't be priced from three-period data. Rates that don't fit your contracted power or yearly consumption are skipped too. Some virtual batteries carry their own fee or expiry; check the provider's terms.
